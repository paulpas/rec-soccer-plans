Open Up — How to Teach It (off the ball)

What it is: An Open Up (aka “check to space,” “open the body,” “open the hips”) is a move made before the ball arrives — with no ball in your feet — to turn your body so your first touch has somewhere good to go. You can’t do it if you’re standing still facing the pass.

Why it exists: If a kid receives facing their defender (or back to the game) and kills the ball dead at their feet, they’re stopped — so the defender takes it. Open up to space first, and the first touch naturally sends the ball into that space while you’re already moving — head up, ready to play.

The four parts (chain together)
  1. Check your shoulder — before the pass, look over your shoulder: where do I want to go, where’s the defender? The look is the trigger.
  2. Open the hips — your hip is a hinge. Swing the leg on the going side and rotate your shoulders to face the open space. Picture opening a door with your body; your back foot stays the hinge.
  3. The check step — your back foot (toward the ball/defender) takes one big step out into the open space as you turn. This “checks” you in and gives you forward momentum.
  4. Quick prep feet — two small quick steps to balance and set your receiving foot (open it, inside out, like a flat target).

In one motion: quick feet → big step opening your hips to face space → receive and send the ball into that space in one touch.

Cues that work with U8
  • Open up like a door.
  • Quick feet, then a big step to space.
  • Check your shoulder, open your hips.
  • Where do you want to go? Turn to it.
  • Receive on the running foot.
Watch for these mistakes
  • Freezing into a stand instead of opening.
  • Facing the defender / back to play.
  • Stopping the ball at their feet instead of sending it into space.
  • Head down — not looking/deciding before it arrives.
Ball-free progression (run this first, no ball)
  1. Mirror / coach-calls — coach calls a direction or points to a cone (“left!”, “behind you!”); kids explode open to face it.
  2. Add a foot — coach says a foot (“left!”); kids open and present that foot ready.
  3. Add a pass — coach feeds; receiver opens first, then receives into that space.
  4. Add pressure — a defender presses; receiver must open away from them.

Gather the team and name the problem plainly: “Today we fix the first touch. When the ball comes at us, do we stop it at our feet — which lets the defender take it — or do we send it to the other foot and pass back in one motion?” Demo: receive a pass, stop it (it’s gone), then receive and tap it to the other foot and pass back in one motion (we keep it). Game: Winner Stays
0/4

2 small goals across a half-field. For 6: 3v3 to a small goal (everyone on the field) — or 2v2 with two rotating, winner-stays. The attacker receives the pass heading toward the defender and MUST receive into space and turn to score. If they keep the ball / score they stay on; the loser passes to the next challenger. Winner-stays = constant pressure, so the first touch into space is the whole game.
